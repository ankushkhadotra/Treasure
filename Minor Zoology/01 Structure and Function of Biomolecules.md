# 1.1 <u>Carbohydrates</U>

- Polyhydroxy aldehyde(-CHO) or ketnone(=CO) derevatives of carbon are called **carbohydrates**.
- Carbohydrate  ----------> Energy + Structure 
- Protein & Nucleic acid ------------> Informational molecule
- DNA  -----Transcription-----> mRNA -------Translation------> ==Protein== (perform all functions in the body.)
## 1.1.1 <u>Types of Carbohydrates</u>

1. On the basis of unit formed after hydrolysis
     1. Monosaccharides(==Sweet sugar==)
	    - These are the single unit of poly hydroxy aldehyde or ketone derevative of carbon.
	    - eg: Glyceraldehyde, Dihydroxy aldehyde, Manose, Galactose, etc.
	2. Oligosaccharides(==Sweet sugar==)
		- These are the carbohydrates which upon hydrolysis produces 2-10 monosaccharides
		- eg: Maltose, Sucrose, Lactose, etc.
	3. Polysaccharides(==Non-Sweet sugar==)
		- Upon hydrolysis they produce more then 10 monosaccharides units
		- eg: Cellulose, Chitin, etc.
2. On the basis of no. of 'C'
	- n=3 ----> Triose
	- n=4 ----> Tetrose
	- n=5 ----> Pentose
	- n=6 ----> Hexose
	- n=7 ----> Heptose
3. On the basis of nature of functional group 
	1. Aldose
		- These are carbohydrates which contain aldehyde
		- eg: Glucose
	2. Ketose
		- These are carbohydrates which contain ketone
		- eg: Fructose

### Trioses (3 carbon)
1. Glyceraldehyde
$$  
\begin{array}{ccc}  
\large\textbf{L-Glyceraldehyde} & \quad\quad\quad\quad & \large\textbf{D-Glyceraldehyde} \\  
\text{( -OH on the Left )} & & \text{( -OH on the Right )} \\  
\\  
\text{CHO} & & \text{CHO} \\  
\vert & & \vert \\  
\color{crimson}{\text{HO}} - \text{C}_2 - \text{H} & & \text{H} - \text{C}_2 - \color{teal}{\text{OH}} \\  
\vert & & \vert \\  
\text{H} - \text{C}_3 - \text{OH} & & \text{H} - \text{C}_3 - \text{OH} \\  
\vert & & \vert \\  
\text{H} & & \text{H} \\  
\end{array}  
$$  
  
> [!tip] **The D/L Rule**  
> * **D-isomer:** The $-OH$ group on the highest-numbered chiral carbon ($C_2$ here) points to the **Right**.  
> * **L-isomer:** The $-OH$ group on the highest-numbered chiral carbon points to the **Left** (L = Left).

2. Dihydroxy acetone (==Achiral==)
   $$  
\begin{array}{}
\large\textbf{Dihydroxyacetone} \\  
\text{(Achiral / Optically Inactive)} \\
\\
\text{H} \\  
\vert \\  
\text{H} - \text{C}_1 - \text{OH} \\  
\vert \\  
\text{C}_2 = \text{O} \\  
\vert \\  
\text{H} - \text{C}_3 - \text{OH} \\  
\vert \\  
\text{H}  
\end{array}  
$$  
  
> [!warning] **Key Difference from Glyceraldehyde**  
> * Dihydroxyacetone is a **ketotriose** (contains a central ketone group $\text{C}=\text{O}$ at $\text{C}_2$).  
> * Because no single carbon is bonded to four *different* groups, it has **no chiral center** and **no D/L enantiomers**.

## Hexoses (6-Carbon Sugars)  

###  Glucose (C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub>) — *Blood Sugar*  
> [!info] Primary Function  
> Main energy source for animals.    
  
### Structural Representations :
  
#### 1. Linear Structure (Fischer Projection)  
The open-chain form showing the aldehyde group at \(C_1\) and consecutive chiral centers.  
  
$$  
\begin{array}{c}  
\text{H} - \text{C} = \text{O} \\  
| \\  
\text{H} - \text{C} - \text{OH} \\  
| \\  
\text{HO} - \text{C} - \text{H} \\  
| \\  
\text{H} - \text{C} - \text{OH} \\  
| \\  
\text{H} - \text{C} - \text{OH} \\  
| \\  
\text{CH}_2\text{OH}  
\end{array}  
$$
* **(C<sub>1</sub>)**: Aldehyde group (\(-CHO\))  
* **Chiral Carbons**: (C<sub>2</sub>, C<sub>3</sub>, C<sub>4</sub>, C<sub>5</sub>) (Asymmetric centers)  
* **Penultimate Carbon**: (C<sub>5</sub>) (Determines D/L configuration)  
* **Ultimate Carbon**: (C<sub>6</sub>) (Final carbon in the chain)  

---  
  
#### 2. Cyclic Structure (Haworth Projection)  
When the linear chain folds, the hydroxyl group on **(C<sub>5</sub>)** attacks the aldehyde carbon at **(C<sub>1</sub>)**, sealing it into a 6-membered pyranose ring. \(C_1\) turns into the **Anomeric Carbon**, generating two dynamic isomers (Anomers):  
  
#### $\alpha$-D-Glucose Structure  
The hydroxyl group on the anomeric carbon (C<sub>1</sub>) points **below** the ring.  
  
```smiles
O[C@@H]1[C@@H](O)[C@H](O)[C@@H](O)[C@@H](CO)O1

OCC1OC(O)C(O)C(O)C1O

```

  
####  $\beta$-D-Glucose Structure  
The hydroxyl group on the anomeric carbon (\(C_1\)) points **above** the ring.  

```smiles
O[C@H]1[C@@H](O)[C@H](O)[C@@H](O)[C@@H](CO)O1

C1(O)C(O)C(O)C(O)C(CO)O1
```

  
---  
  
> [!important] 🧪 Chemical Property: Reducing Sugars  
> **All monosaccharides are reducing sugars.**  
> This is because their $-OH$ group at the anomeric carbon ($C_1$) is **free** (it does not form any glycosidic bond), allowing the ring to open back up into its reactive linear aldehyde form to reduce surrounding reagents.
